0.3.17: keep both leaves of a spread, reject a quad with a lost corner

Two detector changes, each triggered by a failure mode measured on the fresh 42-photo
holdout, and each verified against the untouched 116-photo owner corpus.

1. M-LSD merge-repair no longer cuts a two-page spread in half.

   The owner's rule (2026-11-15): when both leaves are visible and centred the two pages are
   ONE scan; it is a single page only when the facing leaf is under ~25 % visible. The repair
   rule predated that and was tuned the other way round ("detect the only page fully seen"),
   with SPREAD_MAX_STRIP_FRAC widened to 0.55 precisely so a ~50 % gutter could be found.

   Measured on the holdout: 29 of 42 photos were being split, and the strip fractions fell
   into two clean populations - 0.4-0.5 (equal halves: a spread being cut in two) and under
   0.25 (a genuine sliver). A fold may now only remove a sliver smaller than a quarter of the
   half it keeps (MlsdMergeRepair.SECOND_LEAF_KEEP_RATIO), enforced in the selection rule and
   again in MlsdTrigger.acceptsRepair.

2. isPlausiblePageQuad rejects a quad with a blunt vertex.

   A corner pulled deep inside the page (the heat-map peak snapping onto a text block) leaves
   a quad that is nearly a triangle, which shows up as one very wide interior angle far more
   sharply than in area, fill or aspect. Measured: the four degenerate holdout quads sit at
   126.8-133.2 degrees, while every accepted quad on the owner corpus is at or below 123.2.
   Threshold 125 degrees, in the gap between them.

   MIN_QUAD_FILL was deliberately NOT raised to catch the same cases: the degenerates measure
   0.66-0.72 fill but correct owner photos go down to 0.637, so raising it would cost real
   detections.

Measured result
---------------
owner110 (116 photos, unchanged SAM oracle, so this is a pure detector comparison):

    quad is the page (IoU >= 0.5)   44/94 (47 %)  ->  53/94 (56 %)
    mean IoU                         0.481       ->  0.529
    detected                        104          ->  104   (unchanged)
    signature "page"                 19          ->  26
    signature "mislocated"           37          ->  23

No photo lost its detection and the fallback/corner-model split is unchanged
(79 CORNER_MODEL / 24 CLASSICAL_FALLBACK).

holdout42 (42 photos never used for tuning), scored against a SAM page mask:

    locked the page (IoU >= 0.85)     7/42 (17 %)  ->  13/42 (31 %)
    mean IoU                          0.637        ->  0.708
    mean recall                       0.672        ->  0.746
    mean precision                    0.902        ->  0.896

14 photos improved, 3 got worse, 25 are unchanged. The three regressions are
`192342`, `192347` and `192441`: these are the degenerate quads the new angle gate
rejects, after which the classical fallback returns a different (smaller) rectangle. The
gate is doing its job - those quads were geometrically broken - but the fallback that
takes over is not yet better than the broken quad was. That is a corner-accuracy problem.

Scoring note: the holdout's SAM masks had to be repaired before they could be used as a
referee. The first pass selected the mask containing the frame CENTRE, which on these
photos is the table, and `fill_holes` inverted whenever the mask touched pixel (0,0);
8 of 42 masks were desk. Selection is now on "printed matter" (share of the mask sitting
on image edges: desk <= 0.013, pages >= 0.056), and a sheet cut by the frame is treated as
only partly seen, which is the owner's "<25 % visible" case.

Unit tests: the 7 tests that asserted the old "split a balanced spread" behaviour were
rewritten to assert the owner's rule, including both sides of the 25 % boundary. 47 tests,
0 failures.

What this does NOT fix
----------------------
Under-covering is still the dominant holdout failure: on 21 photos the quad sits inside the
page (precision 0.90, recall 0.75) rather than on its edge. That is a corner-accuracy
problem, not a page-choice one, and is the next target.
