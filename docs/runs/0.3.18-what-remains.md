# What is actually left after 0.3.18

Detector build 0.3.18, measured on the 42-photo holdout against a SAM page mask.
Instruments: `h60_remaining.py`, `h61b_leaf.py`, `h62_referee.py`, `h55_quad_vs_mask_sheet.py`.

Raw score: **23 / 42 locked (55 %)**, mean IoU 0.756, 11 UNDER, 3 OVER, 5 PART.

The raw number is **not** the error rate, because part of the gap is the measuring mask, not the
detector. This document separates the two.

## 1. The measuring mask over-covers the page behind (5 of 16 short photos)

`h62_referee.py` measures *where* the uncovered mask lies. On 5 photos the miss is concentrated
**above** the front page (55-100 % of all missed pixels):

| photo | share of miss above the quad |
|---|---|
| `20261115_192445` | 1.00 |
| `20261115_192352` | 0.86 |
| `20261115_192356` | 0.82 |
| `20261115_192455` | 0.65 |
| `20261115_192351` | 0.64 |

In those photos the open book is a fanned stack: the front page is what the user is reading, and
SAM's mask also includes the next page angled up behind it. The detector quad sits correctly on
the front page and is scored as a 65-83 % recall failure.

Confirmed by eye on `192455`: green covers the two pages behind the spread, red correctly covers
the visible open pages.

So at least 5 of the 16 short photos are **referee error, not detector error**. The real detector
miss rate is 11 of 42, not 16.

**Why the mask was not fixed to catch this:** the discriminator is "is the extra region a facing
leaf or a page behind?" - and direction does not work, because several genuine spreads here are
photographed rotated 90 degrees (`192347`, `192348` are real spreads whose leaves differ
*vertically*). The one signal that did separate them was frame truncation (stacks are cut by the
frame, spreads are not), and that is already implemented. These 5 pass that test, so the
remaining over-coverage needs something better than a geometric rule - most likely a real
two-leaf page segmentation rather than point-prompted SAM.

## 2. "Extend across the fold" would fix almost nothing (1 of 42)

`h61b_leaf.py` asks which short photos are a *one-leaf lock on a genuine two-leaf target* - the
only case an extend rule could fix. Criteria: the target really has two leaves
(`pointgrid.spread`), the quad is 40-62 % of it, and precision >= 0.95 (so the quad is exactly on
one leaf).

Result: **1 photo** (`20261115_192347`). One photo does not justify a rule that risks the
over-cover failure 0.3.17 just removed, so it was not written.

`h61_extend.py` additionally looked for a gutter signature (dark valley then paper) outside the
short side: **0 of 16** photos have one. These workbook spreads meet at a flat fold with no
visible gutter, so the classic "find the dark valley" approach does not apply here at all.

## 3. Genuine remaining detector failures

After removing the 5 referee cases, the real misses are:

* **under-cover** - the quad sits inside the paper, a uniform few percent short per side
  (`192340`, `192354_0`, `192407`, `192334`, `192357`, `192350`). This is corner accuracy.
* **inner panel** - a white sticker sheet lying on / beside the workbook cover is locked instead
  of the cover (`192442`, `192444`). Both have precision ~1.00, so the quad is a real rectangle,
  just the wrong one. On `192442` the quad sits on the sticker panel and the green cover shows
  around it; on `192444` the quad locks the sticker panel on the LEFT while the printed cover
  sits untouched on the right. Note both panels are physically separate objects lying on the
  book, so which one is "the page" is genuinely ambiguous in `192444` - it may not be a detector
  error at all, and it should not be counted as one without deciding that question.
* **over-cover** (`192319`, `192354`, `192450`) - all three are CLASSICAL_FALLBACK, where the
  quad takes in table around the page.

`h63_timing.py`: detection is now median 39 ms (holdout) / 51 ms (owner110) after 0.3.18 removed
the unused M-LSD pass.

## What would actually move the number

1. **A better page segmenter for the referee first.** With 5 of 16 "failures" being the mask,
   any detector change is being scored against noise. A two-leaf-aware segmentation would make
   the measurement trustworthy before more tuning.
2. **Inner-panel rejection** is not solved. The obvious signal - "is there more sheet just outside
   this quad?" - was measured with `tooling/h65_enclosure.py` and **does not work**: quads that
   correctly lock a page show a paper-like ring at the same rate as the panel cases (ring/interior
   luma 0.84-1.05 in both), because a page on a stack of paper also has paper outside it. Any rule
   built on that signal would reject correct detections. The remaining options are a real
   page-vs-panel segmentation, or requiring the quad to be the LARGEST sheet in the frame - and
   the second needs the same segmentation to know what "largest sheet" means.
3. **Corner accuracy for the under-cover group** - and note the M-LSD repair that used to touch
   this was measured harmful and is off, so this needs a different mechanism (sub-pixel edge fit
   on the page's own boundary, which already exists as `refineCornersSubpixel`).

## Not claimed

Everything here was measured by replaying photos through the real detector on an emulator. No
physical phone camera was involved. The absolute IoU values depend on the SAM mask quality; the
*before/after* comparisons in `0.3.17-detector-fixes.md` and `0.3.17-repair-disabled.md` are the
reliable part.
